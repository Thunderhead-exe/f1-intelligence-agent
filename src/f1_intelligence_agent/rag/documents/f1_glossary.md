# F1 Glossary

Grand Prix: A Formula 1 race weekend at a specific circuit. It can include practice, qualifying, sprint sessions, and the race.

Session: A timed activity during the weekend, such as FP1, FP2, FP3, qualifying, sprint qualifying, sprint, or race.

Qualifying: A timed session used to decide the starting order. Laps are usually interpreted as push laps, cooldown laps, out laps, or in laps.

Race: The main competitive session. Lap times can be affected by fuel load, tyre age, traffic, safety cars, weather, and strategy.

Sector: One of three timing sections around a lap. Sector deltas help localize where time was gained or lost.

Stint: A continuous run on a tyre set between pit stops. Comparing laps within the same stint is often more meaningful than comparing unrelated laps.

Pit stop: A visit to pit lane, commonly for tyres. Pit-in and pit-out laps should be interpreted separately from normal racing laps.

Tyre compound: The tyre type, such as soft, medium, hard, intermediate, or wet. Compound changes can affect lap time and telemetry behavior.

Tyre life: Approximate laps completed on the tyre set. It can be a useful degradation proxy, but it does not prove a cause by itself.

DRS: Drag Reduction System. When active, it can increase straight-line speed in designated zones.

Speed trap: A fixed timing point where car speed is recorded.

Push lap: A lap that appears to be driven near maximum pace. In qualifying, these are often the main laps to compare.

Cooldown lap: A slower lap used to manage tyres, battery, traffic, or preparation for another push lap.

Safety car: A controlled condition that slows the field after incidents or hazards.

Yellow flag: A caution condition requiring drivers to slow and be prepared to avoid hazards.

Red flag: A stoppage of the session, usually for safety or track condition reasons.

Deleted lap: A lap invalidated by officials, often for track limits. Deleted laps should remain visible but be treated carefully in model interpretation.
